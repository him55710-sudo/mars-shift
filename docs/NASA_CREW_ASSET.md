# NASA-derived crew visualization

The detailed walking figure reuses [NASA's Extravehicular Mobility Unit 3D model](https://science.nasa.gov/3d-resources/extravehicular-mobility-unit/), credited to **NASA / Michael D. Carbajal**, from the [official NASA GitHub mirror](https://github.com/nasa/NASA-3D-Resources/tree/master/3D%20Models/Extravehicular%20Mobility%20Unit). It replaces the earlier primitive suit after loading. It is an adapted visualization of a historical EMU model, not an observed astronaut on Mars, a current Mars suit design or validated EVA biomechanics. Use follows the [NASA Images and Media Usage Guidelines](https://www.nasa.gov/nasa-brand-center/images-and-media/); NASA does not endorse this project.

`scripts/prepare_nasa_crew.py` runs with the installed Blender 5.2.2 LTS in background mode. It downloads the GLB as data when absent, imports the real NASA geometry, removes redundant internal visor layers, unions overlapping legacy shells at a 4.5 mm **asset processing** scale, and reduces polygons. The 4.5 mm value describes the synthetic character asset; it says nothing about terrain resolution. Seven consolidated PBR materials retain the white pressure suit, gold visor, dark seals, metal hardware and colored safety bands. Branded texture patches are removed. The original space-only external maneuvering frame is removed, its rear cut capped, and a compact beveled illustrative PLSS pack is added between shoulder and waist. The compact pack has a padded cover, inset equipment panel, perimeter channels, small service latches, an unbranded safety band and visible illustrative hoses. Twelve deterministic hemisphere probes per vertex bake local geometric occlusion at a 14 cm asset scale into glTF COLOR_0. This emphasizes existing suit creases and hardware contacts without a screen-space ambient-occlusion pass or additional image textures. The probes operate only on the character mesh. These are disclosed visual adaptations.

The source contains **343,455 triangles** in 25 material slots. The delivered GLB contains **29,364 triangles**, **seven material primitives** and **nine bones**, with approximately 14,762 Blender vertices. The GLB is 1,216,908 bytes; exact source/output sizes and SHA256 identities are in `public/models/nasa-emu-web.json`. No runtime Draco decoder is required. Its neutral rendered height is approximately 1.85 m, sole height differs from zero by less than 0.1 mm after processing, and local −Z faces forward in Three.js.

The nine-bone skin and procedural walk are illustrative. The legs and arms receive fitted regional weights; rear life-support hardware remains attached to the torso. The animation is driven by traveled distance. A short frame-time-based easing transition returns the limbs to rest; after settling, a stationary pose needs no redraw. Relaxed shoulders lower the hands from the source pose, and a small fitted hip rotation widens the neutral stance. Eight boot anchors provide a bounded local support-plane correction. This keeps the illustrated stance from visibly floating on level ground, but does not implement independent foot collision on sloping terrain. It does not model suit joint forces, physiological effort or collision clearance. Terrain collision and route planning continue to use the DEM and existing planning masks.

`createAstronautAvatar(loadDetailed = true)` retains the synchronous `{group, update}` interface (`update` returns whether the pose changed and accepts optional elapsed seconds) and adds `load()`, `ready` and `dispose()`. Passing `false` skips model decoding and requests in orbit-only scenes. `load()` is idempotent. Once its promise resolves, render the scene so a standing figure also updates without movement. Disposal removes and frees loaded/fallback geometry, materials and skeletons; a late GLTF response is disposed rather than attached to a departed scene. Network failure retains the small illustrative fallback.

Reproduce with:

```powershell
& 'C:\Program Files\Blender Foundation\Blender 5.2\blender.exe' --background --factory-startup --python scripts/prepare_nasa_crew.py
```

The source download and reviewed front/back preview renders are outside the repository under `../research/realism/`. An editable `MARS-SHIFT-NASA-EMU-v15.blend` is saved under `../deliverables/`. The script starts an isolated empty background scene and does not modify a user's open Blender scene. The GLB was parsed with the same Three.js GLTFLoader used by the app; seven meshes, 29,364 triangles and nine bones were verified, along with physical bounds and successful TypeScript compilation of the avatar module. Front/back Blender renders were visually inspected.


The vertex-color export is explicit: material base-color factors multiply `CrewOcclusion` in `COLOR_0`. The Blender preview uses the equivalent material node graph. Unused legacy UV layers and duplicate faces are removed before export. This is important because a generic preview MixRGB node otherwise drops base colors in the glTF exporter. No Draco decoder or image decoder is required for the compact web asset.


Validation for v15: the actual GLB parses with Three.js GLTFLoader into seven skinned material primitives, nine bones and 29,364 triangles. All seven primitives expose COLOR_0 and retain their material tint factors. Bounds are 1.850001 m high with the neutral sole at −0.000063 m. A 120-sample walk cycle, with the group translated 10 m and yawed 0.6 radians, kept the lowest skinned boot vertex between −0.0020 m and +0.0066 m of the local support plane. The 60 Hz stop transition settled after 35 updates, after which `update` returned false. Disposal removed all loaded children. These checks validate rendering behavior, not suit biomechanics or independently colliding feet.
